🏝 Dhanushkodi History

Dhanushkodi is a ghost town located at the southeastern tip of Pamban Island in Tamil Nadu, India. It lies between the Indian Ocean and the Bay of Bengal and is known for its scenic beauty and historical significance.

According to Hindu mythology, Dhanushkodi is believed to be the place where Lord Rama built the Ram Setu (Adam’s Bridge) to reach Sri Lanka in the Ramayana. The name "Dhanushkodi" means "end of the bow," referring to Lord Rama's bow.

The town was once a thriving place with a railway station, post office, and residential areas. However, in 1964, a devastating cyclone struck the region, destroying the entire town and killing many people. After this disaster, Dhanushkodi was declared unfit for habitation.

Today, Dhanushkodi remains an abandoned town, attracting tourists for its ruins, beaches, and unique landscape where two seas meet. It is often referred to as one of the most mysterious and beautiful places in India.

📌 Key Facts:
  • Located at the tip of Pamban Island, Tamil Nadu
  • Known as a ghost town after the 1964 cyclone
  • Associated with Ramayana and Ram Setu
  • Point where Bay of Bengal meets Indian Ocean
  • Popular tourist spot for ruins and beaches
🧭 Spiritual & Historical Significance:
