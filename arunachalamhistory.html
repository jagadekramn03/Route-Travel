🛕 Arunachalam History

Arunachalam, located in Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu, is one of the most sacred spiritual places in India. It is dedicated to Lord Shiva in the form of Arunachaleswarar, representing the fire element (Agni Lingam) among the Pancha Bhoota temples.

According to Hindu mythology, Lord Shiva appeared as an infinite column of fire to settle a dispute between Lord Brahma and Lord Vishnu. This divine manifestation is believed to be Arunachala hill itself, making the entire hill sacred.

The Arunachaleswarar Temple was developed over centuries by various dynasties such as the Cholas, Pallavas, and Vijayanagara kings. It is one of the largest temples in India, known for its massive gopurams (towering gateways).

The place gained global recognition due to Sri Ramana Maharshi, a great spiritual sage who lived here and spread the teachings of self-realization. His ashram continues to attract spiritual seekers from around the world.

The practice of Girivalam (circumambulation of the hill) is considered highly sacred. Devotees walk around the 14 km path, especially during full moon nights, believing it brings spiritual benefits and peace.

📌 Key Facts:
  • One of the Pancha Bhoota temples (Fire element)
  • Located in Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu
  • Arunachala hill is considered divine
  • Famous for Girivalam (14 km path)
  • Home to Sri Ramana Maharshi Ashram
🧭 Spiritual Significance:
  • Represents Lord Shiva as fire (Agni)
  • Important center for meditation and self-realization
  • Girivalam believed to remove sins and bring peace
📅 Important Timeline:
  • Ancient times – Mythological origin linked to Shiva
  • Chola period – Temple construction and expansion
  • Medieval period – Contributions by South Indian dynasties
  • 20th century – Sri Ramana Maharshi’s influence
  • Present – Major spiritual and pilgrimage destination
