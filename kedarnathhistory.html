🛕 Kedarnath History

Kedarnath Temple is one of the holiest shrines dedicated to Lord Shiva and is part of the Char Dham pilgrimage. It is situated in the Garhwal Himalayas near the Mandakini River in Uttarakhand.

According to Hindu mythology, after the Mahabharata war, the Pandavas sought forgiveness from Lord Shiva for the sins committed during the war. Lord Shiva avoided them and took the form of a bull. When the Pandavas tried to capture him, he disappeared into the ground, leaving behind his hump at Kedarnath. This hump is worshipped as the main idol in the temple.

The temple is believed to have been originally built by the Pandavas and later revived by Adi Shankaracharya in the 8th century. He also established the Char Dham pilgrimage route to promote spiritual unity across India.

Kedarnath is located at an altitude of about 3,583 meters (11,755 feet), making it one of the highest Shiva temples in the world. Due to extreme weather conditions, the temple remains open only for six months (April/May to October/November). During winter, the idol is moved to Ukhimath for worship.

In 2013, Kedarnath witnessed devastating floods caused by cloudbursts and glacial lake outburst. The town suffered massive destruction, but the temple structure remained almost untouched, which many consider a miracle.

📌 Key Facts:
  • One of the 12 Jyotirlingas of Lord Shiva
  • Located at 3,583 meters altitude
  • Part of Char Dham Yatra
  • Open only 6 months a year
  • Survived the 2013 floods
  • 16 km trek from Gaurikund
🧭 Spiritual Significance:
  • Represents Lord Shiva’s power and forgiveness
  • Visited by lakhs of pilgrims every year
  • Considered a path to moksha (liberation)
📅 Important Timeline:
  • Ancient period – Temple believed built by Pandavas
  • 8th century – Revived by Adi Shankaracharya
  • 2013 – Major floods hit Kedarnath region
  • Present – One of the most visited pilgrimage sites in India
